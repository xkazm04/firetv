# R3 — The library offered as choices

This wave builds three original composer candidates for each of the 34 campaign event slots requiring redesign; `switchback-4` retains accepted Runoff. All 35 event identifiers are the immutable review keys. A candidate is a proposal, not an owner Keep. R0, R1 and R2 were committed and green before this wave started. **R3 is partial:** all 102 choices pass the six-car lap-rehearsal contract, but the three finale arenas still fail actual two-car duel resolution. Deliberate hunter blocking and the busy shared Stick also remain unproved. The review exposes these limits instead of marking the whole library mastered.

The proposed theme openings are industrial (scrap), quarry (foundry), desert (salt), alpine (switchback) and wetland (crown). Mid visits can cross themes. Qualifiers provide a longer final in each of those themes; the four boss visits and final elimination arena have their own choices. Changing a proposed course assignment does not change a story/event ID. Existing production CSVs remain intact until a choice is made; review exports include the proposed assignment and lap count.

The old lap counts were derived for the rejected geometry. The new proposals use fewer, longer laps where necessary. No short race is repaired by increasing its lap count. Opening duration is measured at the event's car tier against 120–180 seconds; final targets are 240–360 seconds, with greater road length and corner complexity. Both solo reference and actual six-car outcomes are reported; a wreck, watchdog or timeout is not a finish-time success.

Candidate acceptance requires physical lint, the R1 shape gates and pairwise outline distinctness, followed by six-car evidence. Shape tuning changes road geometry, widths, surfaces, pickup placement and the designated grid; it does not retune vehicle or combat balance. Seed/identity rotation, stuck cars, early lead-slot wrecks, positions, contact and actual hunter-intent damage remain visible. Owner feel remains unmeasured.

## Design and rejection process

The author works from original anchor/primitive families: hooks, offset ladders, nested returns, angled rally sequences, courtyard routes, stepped ribbons, crossed hooks and split/rejoin roads. Independent bays and S sequences change the corner order; rotation, reflection and uniform scaling do not count as distinct designs. The compiler emits the existing centreline/width/surface format. Every exported draft carries its geometry recipe, actual final CSVs, spans, measured baked geometry, theme lesson and proposed campaign assignment.

The first non-oval pass still repeated too many E and U silhouettes. I rendered and personally inspected the sheets, rejected 38 choices, tightened global normalized outline distance from 0.055 to 0.14 and signed-turn distance to 0.10, and regenerated against the retained library. A second inspection rejected five same-slot U siblings even where the numerical comparison permitted them. Pacing edits later created five failing pairs; four further choices were regenerated. These are rejected designs, not owner ratings. Archives preserve recipes, outlines and reasons. A stubborn salt mid-course also required a new layout after several grid/recovery experiments failed the unchanged fairness gate.

Every new outline must pass the R1 compactness, direction-reversal, fold-back, corner-family and straight/brake gates. Every pair must clear the similarity thresholds, including mirrored and reversed comparisons. Finals and boss visits additionally require at least eight corners and 1,200 degrees of absolute turning. The accepted original Runoff is excluded from the new shape contract and remains unchanged.

A final post-pacing audit caught two Foundry courses whose stretched bends no longer met the hairpin gate. Their physical lint and simulation results alone had not detected that loss. I restored the tight returns, added independent outward bays, and reran shape, pair and full 72-trial checks. Both proof and packing now enforce the shape contract before accepting any result. The final sheets were rendered again and directly inspected at 12:58 UTC, including these corrected bays. All 102 choices pass; there are zero flagged outline pairs. Eighteen design families remain recognizable, so this is measured distinctness plus a visual review, not a claim of 102 unrelated topologies.

Widths are measured in the actual widest car's 4.65 m body width (W); lengths and radii use the longest 9.3 m car (L). The initial technical road is 3.7 W, with 4.3 W releases and 5.15 W passing areas. Some measured compression failures required 4.15 W technical releases. These are full ribbon widths, not half-widths. The proposal does not shrink cars or change the physical scale contract. The research's typical road-car comparison and this game's unusually large maximum body are different denominators.

Existing theme surfaces, tyres, trees, pickup graphics and warning props supply the landmarks. Where a safe straight is available, a surface change receives an existing prop 45 m before it. Gravel shortcuts preserve a distance/grip tradeoff; declared junctions and the finale split are actual runtime roads. The camera and art remain unchanged. An outline sheet cannot establish sofa readability or human feel; those remain unmeasured.

## Simulation contract and fixes exposed by it

Each final candidate receives 12 seeds × six rotations of car/style identities through the six-car grid, at the event's actual stock tier with Pro AI and representative rival styles. This is a controlled track comparison, not exhaustive coverage of every purchased loadout or named campaign boss configuration. Three additional clean six-car runs must finish every car without a three-second stall. Combat trials separately measure passes, contacts, spins, wrecks, temporary stalls, racing lines, hunter intent, early lead-slot wrecks and unresolved cars. A recovered combat stall is reported; it is not silently described as zero stalls. The first and second seed halves and all identity rotations have separate descriptive checks. The lead-slot AI is a surrogate for fairness, not a human test.

Both clean median finish time and actual lap-winner median must meet the role's time band. At least 80% of lap-race trials must produce a lap winner. Elimination instead of laps is reported separately. Arena six-car rehearsals test two laps; their campaign exports retain zero lap target and elimination rules. Exact repeated state/trajectory hashes and a 90-degree rotated reference provide independent replay/orientation checks. The ordinary comparison uses the existing instrument's damage scale 1.0, also used by the Lab, rather than the campaign divisions' 0.10–0.16 encounter multipliers; it is a controlled high-pressure comparison, not a complete campaign playthrough.

The initial arena test mixed the supplied basic rig with five tier-four opponents in a six-car elimination. That is an artificial stress field, not the real finale: `campaign.csv` and `CampaignRules` specify a two-car duel. All three artificial stress fields failed; their complete 72-run records are preserved under `evidence/tracks/r3/arena-initial`. The delivered page therefore separates equal-tier six-car lap rehearsal from the actual campaign duel. The latter uses `RivalEconomy.apply`, the named boss pursuit style, the supplied rig, damage scale 0.65 and the existing canonical 8,000-credit duel-test fixture, with both drivers controlled by Pro AI and 12 seeds. Asymmetric roles are not rotated and are not disguised as six cars.

The actual duel must resolve without timeouts before an arena can be called mastered. Narrowing technical sections, adding eight reachable ammunition sites, removing most repairs and testing another grid improved some runs but did not satisfy that condition. The final arena watchdog is the campaign's existing 600 seconds, not a new time-limit win. Current results are 9/12 resolved for A, 10/12 for B and 6/12 for C. C retains one repair site to pass the six-car first-lap fairness check; A and B have none. These are visibly flagged design proposals. Further arena geometry/encounter work is required, rather than another budget increase or a relaxed gate.

Candidate race profiles carry laps, tier and the actual watchdog through the importer, Lab, native preview and ZIP. Slow-tail budgets do not change the winner pacing target. A watchdog expiry is never counted as a win. Several apparently successful longer courses initially failed because the app's old 300/420-second budget expired before the slowest car; explicit course budgets expose and correct that disagreement.

Recovery was a spatial defect as well as a quantity question. Two repair icons at ±3.5 m were both collectible by one centre-running body capsule. The final paired lanes use ±5.2 m, on sufficiently wide clear road, outside solid-obstacle footprints. A regression test uses the actual pickup trigger. Different recovery densities and selected early pairs are authored in `recovery.csv`; the final CSV is authoritative, since the anchor recipe alone does not include these placements. Extra recovery remains a feel tradeoff for the owner.

The shallow finale split exposed a real second-lap bug: projection found the road under an AI car, but its steering route still referred to the other branch after a shove or shallow fork. `World` now keeps every driver's route identity consistent with the physical projection. A six-car, three-seed clean regression completes both laps, alongside the R2 crossing/branch tests. The failed replay is preserved. This changes route bookkeeping, not AI skill or combat tuning.

A final audit also found that the proof cache omitted starting-grid position. The corrected shared digest includes the actual start fraction, all exported CSV data, tier, laps and the route-projection evidence version. A regression test proves that moving a grid invalidates proof. The final evidence is rerun under this contract; stale probes cannot appear as current proof in the owner page.

Focused hunter observations require actual hunting intent toward the current leader, close corner perception, attributed HIT damage and sustained low-speed proximity. Three episodes were observed on two courses in 216 focused trials; one held the leader near 2.2 m/s. Replay coordinates are exported. These are observed pursuit/compression episodes, not a claim of counterfactual causation. A stricter follow-up requiring the runtime's active corner-block speed command found **zero episodes in 288 additional trials** across four courses. Thus deliberate blocking/trapping on multiple courses has not been established and remains work, rather than being inferred from generic contact heat. Generic proximity heat stays labelled as trap opportunities.
