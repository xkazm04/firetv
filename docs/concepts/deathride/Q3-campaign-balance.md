# Q3 - Campaign evidence design

Design before the run, 2026-10-02. Use the current core solver and fresh physical outcomes. The 2,000 seeded career ledgers will resample a disclosed physical library; they are not 2,000 independent end-to-end physical playthroughs. No finishing positions are invented to make a career clear. A career that reaches the 70-race observation limit is censored and keeps its actual event, debt and assets. Bankruptcy, a skill/progression stall, a missed PR target and a watchdog draw are separate results.

The ordinary-race library covers all 34 lap events, every legal current-or-lower-tier chassis, three affordable upgrade bands, three lead decision skills, and four deterministic seeds per cell. Opponents use Club decisions and the canonical rival shop schedule. Their field is a reference trajectory, not a claim that every resampled career owns the same NPC garage. Each ledger still pays its own NPCs through the real settlement and shops. Select library outcomes by the nearest player/field PR ratio, report the approximation gap, and retain the physical seed and outcome in the raw evidence. All cars use the common physics and ability rules.

Compare money, named car and useful part promotion policies across the 2,000 ledgers. Claim through the actual revision-checked transaction, select owned eligible cars through the normal ownership rules, and buy useful parts through the shared shop. No gifts beyond the campaign data, no injected player money, no synthetic boss victory. Report races to each ally, finale arrivals, money actually credited/recovered/voided, conditions, minimum cash, early wrecks and the PR-ratio timeline with boss events marked. The supplied finale rig is a separate power case.

The death-duel cohort uses Rookie, Club and Pro lead decision proxies against the same Marrow, with both front grid slots rotated. The rig and the boss retain identical physical specifications across skill settings. Count player wins divided by all fights, also report resolved-only wins and draws separately. An opening loss is the human-lead proxy wrecked before the 20-second gate; target below 5%. Report durations, mine dispatches/hits, repairs, one-shot count, exact replay checks and state-hash diversity. Reused seeds, constant hashes or a rotation-dependent conclusion are alarms, not extra sample size.

The registry's winner-share, rotation-confound and seed-diversity notes inform the instrument audit. Decision proxies are not measured human skill or enjoyment. Any parameter experiments must keep rejected results, use a fresh acceptance seed namespace, expose the final coefficients in their existing data authority, and rerun the required build/browser checks. Existing G2 boss-dip and H3 frame-tail misses remain open until the new evidence resolves them. Art generation budget for this wave: zero.

Pilot finding and design correction: the first 72 physical boss attempts had zero first-place finishes, even at the legal part cap and Pro decisions. The old schedule put every opponent in the next tier while preventing the player from buying or entering it. Promotion now opens its named next-tier licence when the boss qualifier becomes available (rounds cleared 6/13/20/27), and the event CSV explicitly permits that tier. The boss must still be beaten to join and advance the plot. This grants no money, parts or stats; saving, a normal loan, an older upgraded car and the post-victory stock-car choice remain real alternatives. The ordinary field schedule is unchanged. The rejected partial library is retained, and acceptance physics will restart with the expanded legal entry set. This supersedes the Q0 licence timing assumption, without granting retroactive rewards.

Duel pilots are separately named. Initial lane matching let a chasing rig shoot a waiting boss without passing. A passing-lane decision removed that trap but produced many draws. Waiting speed and lateral space are public campaign-rule data; tuning changes only those decisions. The Mine remains 0.5 m with its original damage, arming, lifetime and deduplication.


## Final duel and instrument findings

Final coefficients: a visible rear target makes Marrow leave 3.5 m of lateral passing space and wait at 0.56 of the normal target speed. Perception stays 80 m with line-of-sight and the existing reaction cadence; encounter damage remains 0.65 for both cars. No engine, HP, weapon radius or player-power boost was added. Rejected 0.72, 0.30 and 0.48 policies and the original lane-matching pilot remain under `evidence/campaign/q3/experiments`.

The fresh acceptance namespace has 512 seed blocks, each run at three decision settings and both lead grid slots: 3,072 physical duels. The aggregate results below contain paired rotations; uncertainty intervals are reported only within each 512-fight rotation cell. A draw stays in the all-fight win denominator and is removed only from resolved winner shares.

No opening loss before 20 seconds and no one-shot kill occurred. Each rotation cell has 512 distinct seeds and 512 distinct end-state hashes. The grid changes the estimated win rate by 4.88, 4.69 and 0.39 percentage points respectively, so both rows are retained in the plot. Marrow wins more resolved fights in every cell; the largest share is about 76.2%, below the declared diagnostic 85% alarm. This is an asymmetric, difficult finale, not symmetric competitive balance. The alarm was chosen during instrument design and tuning, not preregistered externally. Planted 100% winner, 50/50 winner, constant-hash and unique-hash cases verify reachable thresholds and the right denominators.

Higher driving decision settings do **not** monotonically improve this combat outcome. Their overtaking/avoidance changes exposure to guns and mines. These are three existing decision proxies, not a validated ladder of human combat skill. The lower Pro win rate remains an explicit limitation; it is not relabelled as proof of fairness.

A separate 768-fight paired control at seed namespace 777501 removed only the rig dispatcher. With / without it, wins were Rookie 83/61, Club 67/49 and Pro 53/41 out of 256 per setting. The measured improvements are 8.59, 7.03 and 4.69 percentage points in that exploratory cohort. Ordinary weapons, cars, slots and boss rules were identical. This supports a useful dispatcher contribution in the proxy; it does not establish that every win came from a mine or that a player will understand its tactics.

The ordinary library keeps the lead in slot zero, unlike the rotated duel. Its statistics describe the lead entry and early-wreck risk, not every rival's roster dominance. Rival outcomes are retained for real settlements, but no full-roster fairness pass is inferred. The registry notes were read in place and not edited.


## Final career evidence

The accepted library contains **11,520 physical races**, 34 ordinary events and all **1,872** legal event/skill/car/upgrade-band cells: four seeds in ordinary cells and twenty in boss cells. The supplement reproduces the base sweep's reference rival garages byte-for-byte. There were zero observed lead wrecks before lap one and zero unresolved surviving leads. Every cell meets the 99% distinct-hash alarm. Thirty-four event samples replayed their exact hashes on the final core; six duel cell samples also replayed exactly during that sweep.

Two training seeds choose each tier's chassis for the race-informed buyer. Held-out seeds 2/3 supply ordinary outcomes and seeds 2..19 supply boss outcomes. The resulting plans use Needle, Trail, Flint, Quill, then Bulwark for Rookie/Pro or Kestrel for Club. A separate highest-PR buyer uses the existing legal spending policy. Both run **2,000 seeded career ledgers**, split across three lead decision settings and three preferred reward choices. These are real ledger/shop/settlement executions resampling the measured library, not 4,000 independent full-physics careers. Each ledger pays and shops its own NPC garages; outcome selection approximates that changing field with the nearest physical-library PR ratio. The 70-race limit remains unchanged.

Final cancellation therefore voided zero additional debt in these cohorts, although the nonzero cancellation branch is unit-tested. The financial reversal can be small for a grinding player; that narrative/economy limitation remains visible.

### PR curve and approximation limits

The first-entry median player/field ratios below use the arriving population, which changes as runs are censored. The debt plot shows the balance after the first attempt at each event. Neither curve silently fills missing later events with successful careers.

| Boss event | Race-informed median | PR-control median | Historical proposed band |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rook, 7 | 0.8551 | 0.8828 | 0.85-0.90 |
| Ox, 14 | 0.8658 | 0.9095 | 0.85-0.90 |
| Vex, 21 | 0.9908 | 0.9886 | 0.85-0.90 |
| Mica, 28 | 1.0162 | 1.0182 | 0.85-0.90 |

The first two race-informed dips are present; the later dips still fail. G2 already established upper-tier shared-stat/part ceilings that prevent guaranteeing those late ratios through NPC money alone. The new qualifier licence fixes a legal-purchase barrier and allows observed wins; it does not create a wider upper-tier roster. That roster/handling balance work remains open. The supplied finale rig is separate, at about 0.521 player/field PR, and its measured survivor outcomes above replace a lap-ratio target.

Across 107,780 ordinary race-informed attempts, nearest-band PR mismatch is 0.00627 median, 0.08344 p95 and 0.15471 maximum; 263 attempts fall outside the authored 0.80-1.15 envelope. The PR control has 131,993 ordinary attempts, mismatch 0.000044 / 0.06676 / 0.15313, and 1,648 out-of-envelope attempts. Three upgrade bands and two held-out ordinary seeds are coarse; this report must not be used as a precise human win-rate or optimal-spending claim. More physical bands, independently evolved rival economies and human play are the next validation steps. No stronger accuracy claim is made than the library supports.
