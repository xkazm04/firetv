# Owner checks

Feel is certified only by the owner. Automated evidence is under `deathride/evidence/phase1`.

## W1 ? Feel Lab (2026-09-30)

On the Stick open **Death Ride** (`dev.deathride.tv`). Pair from the TV QR. LEFT/RIGHT on the remote changes the session preset live; the phone's preset button opens the settings sheet. Both displays should agree without a race restart. Balanced is a proposal, not a verdict. Spike retains the original steering coefficients; brake now overrides GO.

For **each of Spike, Loose, Agile, Balanced, Stable**, do these three exercises and label each **too twitchy / right / too heavy**:

1. Slow at the end of the straight and make the tightest hairpin you can. Good: achievable thumb travel and a predictable radius. Bad: running out of pad, delayed turn-in, or an unexpected spin.
2. Slalom left/right down the straight. Good: each direction change arrives when expected. Bad: an oscillation grows, or your thumb must wait for the car.
3. Hold a bend, briefly brake, then countersteer and release. Good: the slide can be caught. Bad: snap-back, uncatchable rotation, or a heavy car refusing the requested turn.

Record preset, maneuver, speed, and the three-word rating. Loose deliberately permits more slip; Stable deliberately softens small corrections. Check simultaneous GO+BRAKE stops propulsion and releasing GO coasts immediately.

Optical check: film phone and TV together at 240 fps for at least 30 FLASH TEST taps. Count frame separation ? 1000/240 ms and record p50/p95/max. Good against the proposed rubric: p50 <=120 ms, p95 <=180 ms. Bad: a growing delay or missed flash. Optical latency and physical-phone comfort are **not measured** by the automated network test.

## W2 ? Five cars (2026-09-30)

In the lobby, DOWN cycles P1's car on the TV; each phone's CAR button selects its own car. Try Needle, Line, Bastion, Comet and Trail. Confirm the same name and seven stat bars on phone/TV. Good: selections survive a race/rematch and cannot change mid-race. Bad: the name changes while performance does not, or one phone changes the other car.

Drive the same bend in Trail then Comet, and accelerate in Needle then Bastion. Good: Trail holds the corner; Comet gains speed on a clear straight; Needle launches quickly; Bastion feels slower. Bad: every class feels identical or one is better at everything. Armor and mounts are future combat capacity until W4, not a tested advantage yet. Report car + preset + maneuver; felt verdict **not measured**.

## W3 ? Weight, drift and surfaces (2026-09-30)

Use FEEL ? Practice surface or TV MENU to cycle Asphalt/Gravel/Oil/Ice. Try the same turn on each. Good: low grip needs earlier braking and longer recovery; the edge verge slows you before the wall. Bad: changing surface does nothing, cars jitter, or AI stays stuck for an entire race.

Hold DRIFT while steering at speed, then release and countersteer. The phone says DRIFTING and the car gets a yellow marker. Good: held drift is recoverable; GO release still coasts and backgrounding the phone clears handbrake. Bad: a latched handbrake or uncontrolled spin after straightening. The temporary drift button is revised with combat in W4.

Compare a glancing wall touch with a head-on impact, then bump Bastion with Needle. Good: glancing keeps forward motion; the light car is displaced more. Bad: wall glue, teleportation, interpenetration or a light car throwing the heavy one farther. Owner feel, especially ice difficulty, remains **not measured**.

## W6 - Tracks, larger cars and a readable world (2026-09-30)

The five presets are approved as they stand; no calibration was performed. MENU now cycles **circuits**, replacing its old surface shortcut. Practice surfaces remain in the phone settings. Selecting another circuit restores its authored surface zones.

From the normal sofa position, compare Needle and Bastion in the lobby preview, then drive Foundry Ring. Cars are roughly 50% longer and wider than the interrupted W6 version, including their contact shapes. Good: the nose, tires and class silhouette remain legible at speed; Bastion occupies appreciably more road and pushes a light car aside. Bad: tiny cars, ambiguous heading, overlapping bodies, flickering walls, or a camera that hides the next bend. Two separated phones intentionally widen the shared camera; use the minimap to keep circuit context.

Use CAR > Circuit on the phone (or MENU on the remote) for all five courses. Foundry teaches broad bends; Switchback an eastern hairpin; Redline long fast approaches; Runoff changes asphalt/gravel/ice; Crucible asks for close positioning. Good: distinct layouts, visible surface changes, sequential laps, clean grid starts, and six AIs completing the course. Bad: a missed gate counting a lap, a car permanently stuck, or surfaces that cannot be read. The dark oil spots are live hazards; pickup sites become usable in W4.

Brake into a bend and hold DRIFT: tire marks and dust should show where grip went. Check the large position/lap/speed HUD and the minimap. Good: useful motion cues without covering the road. Bad: clutter, hard-to-read text, or sustained slowdown. Automated Stick checks and screenshots exist; sofa readability, enjoyment and physical-phone feel are **not measured** by them. The visuals are still procedural placeholder work, far from a finished game's variety, animation, lighting and sound.


## W4 - Weapons, wrecks and two-thumb layouts (2026-09-30)

Start a race with Line or Bastion. Weapons arm for four seconds after GO; the HUD counts this down and shots cost no ammunition until armed. Hold FIRE to use Rivet, tap SWAP for Hammer, and hold MINE to drop behind you. Needle has one mount and cannot equip Hammer. Blue roadside crates refill ammo; green crosses repair. Good: a visible forward shot, decreasing ammunition, readable cooldowns, and matching HP on phone/TV. Bad: a mine or swap triggered by sliding off FIRE, a shot during arming, or HP disagreement.

Mines show an amber expanding arming ring, then a red danger zone. Drive around one, then deliberately revisit an armed mine you dropped. Good: a readable warning and one damage event per blast, including self-damage. Bad: invisible hits, rapid repeated damage while a blast lingers, or damage before the mine arms. Damaged cars show scars and smoke; a wreck becomes a dark obstacle, cannot shoot/heal/count more laps, and ranks below surviving cars. Last survivor wins by elimination. A wrecked first phone must not end the second phone's race.

Open the settings button and compare **Classic**, **Cruise**, **Split**, then the left-handed mirror. Classic separates GO/fire and permits coasting attacks. Cruise's FIRE also holds GO. Split uses an upward steering-pad drag for throttle, leaving the other thumb for weapons. Initial pad touch is neutral. Brake overrides drive in all three. Good: steering, fire and release remain independent; opening settings, changing layout, backgrounding or losing the link clears attacks. Bad: cramped reach, accidental acceleration, latched fire/mine, or needing to look down repeatedly. Record layout + mirror + car + what was awkward; these are alternatives for you to choose, not an ergonomic verdict.

Automated Stick tests use desktop Chrome touch emulation. Physical-phone reach, vibration support, perceived combat fairness and enjoyment are **not measured**. The tuned headless sample no longer kills a car in the opening grid pile-up, but it remains a severe combat racer with several wrecks per race. No audio is present; visual telegraphs alone need your judgment.


## W5 - Parts, pit service and saved garages (2026-09-30)

From lobby/results press PLAY/PAUSE on the remote, or GARAGE on the phone. On TV, UP/DOWN selects a part, LEFT/RIGHT selects a car and SELECT buys; BACK returns to the lobby. On phone, pick a car and tap its explicit INSTALL button. Start with Line's 160 CR: brakes cost 120 CR and move Braking from 6 to 7; tires cost 160 CR. Good: the price is clear, cash changes once, the displayed stats match on both screens, and the part stays with that car. Bad: a double tap buys another tier, a class-limit part charges cash, or switching car carries the upgrade across. Armor deliberately trades handling for protection/mass. All eight stats now include Braking; the five approved steering presets are unchanged.

Race, then open the garage: the receipt separates prize, pit service, insurance and money banked. A wreck has insured repairs and positive earnings, and the next race starts repaired. Good: one payout per race and no repair debt blocking the next start. Bad: payouts from lobby demos, payouts when abandoning with BACK, negative money or a result that pays twice. A full wallet caps at 8,000 CR; a fully upgraded garage is finite completion.

Close and reopen the TV app, pair the same browser again, and verify cash, selection, receipt and parts. Browser storage identifies the profile; another browser is another garage, and deleting browser data loses that association. A recovered previous save is visibly labelled; damaged copies must never silently overwrite progress. Physical-phone shop comfort, whether purchases feel valuable, long-term pacing and the owner's quality verdict remain **not measured**. The economic career sweeps are an assumption-based model, not owner play evidence.


## W7 - Career, named rivals and saved progression (2026-09-30)

UP now opens the career from lobby/results; **REWIND now resets pairing** in the lobby. MENU still changes the practice circuit, DOWN chooses the practice car, and PLAY/PAUSE opens the garage. In the career, LEFT/RIGHT changes Rookie/Club/Pro, SELECT starts the next round and BACK returns. On phone use CAREER; the settings sheet now contains FULLSCREEN and FLASH TEST. Pick an unlocked career car on the phone, or use the TV garage to change car.

Try a three-round Yard Cup. Good: named rivals match their class previews, the next event/course and prize range are clear, each eligible result advances once, and the cup shows your points and best medal. Bad: an idle timeout clears a round, a locked car starts a career event, or a result pays its cup bonus twice. A wreck after meaningful forward progress counts; a driver who stays alive needs one completed lap. Surviving without driving still earns the ordinary insured receipt, but no career progress.

Compare Rookie (no rival mines), Club (mines and avoidance), and Pro (also seeks visible repairs when hurt). Good: readable differences in decisions, with the same car physics and damage rules. Bad: hidden speed/HP boosts, unavoidable targeting, or difficulty that feels unfair. The reference-driver model orders win rates as intended; **human fairness and fun are not measured**. The scripted controllers fire and mine aggressively and have wrecked before three laps, so those device runs are not a completed-lap skill verdict.

Player 1 hosts. Player 2 replaces Rook, gets a separate cash/repair receipt, and keeps their own garage; their career does not advance as a guest. Reopen the app and pair the same two browsers again: both profiles must recover independently. Good: host round/credits and guest credits match their prior results. Bad: one phone inherits another's progress. New phones join from menus; an existing token reconnects during a race.

Trail/Bastion/Comet unlock after rounds 2/5/8. Later part tiers unlock at rounds 4/5/8 and cannot be bought by switching to practice; practice retains all five cars/courses for feel checks. Best cup medals, unlocks and parts survive a new season. Value of these rewards, sofa/menu comfort and whether the career makes you want another race remain **not measured**.

## W8 - Complete session and the owner's G1 decision (2026-09-30)

The integrated evidence and limitations are in [G1-REPORT](../docs/concepts/deathride/G1-REPORT.md). G1 has **not** been passed by automation. Start with the installed Death Ride build, pair, choose Line, buy a useful first-tier part, and play the three-round Yard Cup. Good: each result explains your place, pit cost and reward; the next round is obvious; reopening the app and pairing the same browser restores your car, cash and progress. Bad: an unexplained payout, no sense that the part helps, or no interest in another race.

Judge combat before approving art. The final scripted career session had three double wrecks; an earlier camera-corrected run also demonstrated two three-lap finishes. On Rookie, decide whether a loss was avoidable and whether repairs/mines/gun direction are readable without sound. Compare Line and Bastion in Foundry and Crucible practice. Good: armor and contact strength give Bastion a useful role despite its speed cost. Bad: it simply cannot compete. The AI balance report shows that weakness away from the arena. Fully upgraded cars overwhelmingly beat the stock rival roster; decide whether later cups still have enough challenge.

Use two phones, then let one driver wreck. The camera and main HP/lap display now follow the remaining active driver, whose name is shown. Good: the survivor stays large enough to drive and the wreck is still visible on the minimap. Bad: the camera remains centered between a wreck and a live car, or the HUD reports the wrong driver. Home/resume should reconnect both seats with no held throttle, fire or mine; the track must return intact.

Try Classic/Cruise/Split and mirror on actual phones from the sofa. Good: independent releases, comfortable reach and eyes staying on the road. Bad: looking down repeatedly, accidental attacks or a cramped thumb. The automated touch browser is not a physical-phone comfort test. Use the W1 optical recipe for 30 filmed taps; network age is not optical latency. iOS, contended Wi-Fi and physical-phone haptics remain not measured.

Record a G1 verdict with concrete reasons: **worth an art pass / mechanics and presentation need another pass first**. The larger cars, authored courses, procedural cues and full progression loop exist and have run on the Stick. Finished-game quality, fun and perceived fairness have not been certified.
