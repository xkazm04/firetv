# Death Ride: Voice bibles

Head writer's draft, 2026-10-04, for owner sign-off before any synthesis (R3 B6, step 1). Each bible follows R3's C2 template. "Never says" lists are hard rules: breaking one is a defect, checked in [WRITING-PROCESS.md](WRITING-PROCESS.md). Sample lines are taken from `deathride/narrative/lines.csv`.

**Global rules for every voiced line (R3 A13, current model `eleven_multilingual_v2`):** one breath, about 6 to 14 words; numbers, currency and abbreviations spelled out; at most one capitalised word and at most one `...` or dash per line; no v3 audio tags; no stage directions inside the text; caption identical to audio. Names locked for pronunciation: **Marrow** (MARR-oh), **Mica** (MY-kuh), **Rook**, **Ox**, **Vex**, **Relay** (REE-lay), **Tull**, **Ennis** (EN-iss), **Bettany** (BET-uh-nee), **cairn** (KAIRN), **the Crown**. Avoid homographs (lead, wind, tear, live, read). Where "read" cannot be avoided, the past tense is marked in the voice direction.

**Who calls whom what (R3 A4, address):**

| Speaker | Calls the player | Calls the Mechanic | Calls Marrow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marrow | nothing; speaks of "the car", or the chassis | "the boy" | |

| Relay | "driver" | "the mechanic" | "the league" |

## Marrow: the man who keeps the book

```
CHARACTER: Marrow, owner of the league, the road licences and the book. Older man, olive weathered
           skin, silver crest, high black collar, rust mantle, old temple scar. Drives the Bulwark.
CORE (his words): "The road is kept by the book. I keep the book."
HIDES: He needs drivers on paper: a paid driver leaves. He needs the Voice, the only person he does
       not control. He believes, truly, that without him the regions burn the road again.
STATUS MOVE: Waits. Corrects. Never interrupts the Voice; interrupts everyone else to fix a figure.
RHYTHM: 4 to 10 words; complete sentences; full stops only; a beat before the last clause; no
        exclamation marks, ever; at most one question per scene, and only one he knows the answer to.
LEXICON: ledger and property: account, arrangement, entry, figure, registered, terms, settle,
         collateral, depreciation, in good order, noted. Signature words: "noted", "in good order",
         "registered", "the arrangement", "correct", "the book".
         Address: the car, never the driver ("the car is cornering well"); "the boy" for the Mechanic.
GRAMMAR HABITS: No contractions. Passive voice when something is taken ("The car has been
         recorded"). Exact numbers, never rounded (in text; voiced lines avoid figures).
NEVER SAYS: "debt"; "please"; "kill", "die" or "dead"; a direct threat; a raised voice or an
         exclamation; a rounded number; the player's name or number; "I'm sorry".
HUMOUR: None intended. The dry irony comes from precision applied to horror ("He interrupts once,
        to correct a figure").
UNDER PRESSURE: Shorter and more exact. Never louder.
ARC: Courteous creditor (Acts 1-4, through the ledger and notices) -> owner reclaiming property
     (Act 5, on track and at the seizure) -> a man asking his only friend to get one number right
     (finale). What never changes: the calm.
TELLS: Wipes his feet (the only time he enters the shop is the seizure). Sweeps the start line
       himself. Waits for the Voice to finish. Folds receipts before leaving them. Knows chassis
       numbers by heart.
TTS: Not cast yet. Needs a low, unhurried older male voice with a narrow dynamic range; stability
     high (about 0.6), style about 0.2. Never direct it to shout or whisper. "Marrow" pronounced
     MARR-oh. Most of his presence is voiced through the Voice reading "From the book" notices.
```

**His one argument, said once, to Mica in her hut:** "Before the book, they burned convoys on this pass. Nobody burns a convoy that owes them money." It is a trade, not a lecture: he wants her neutrality.

**What makes him frightening:** he is reasonable. Every line sounds like fairness, so the listener feels in the wrong (Graeber, via R4 P6). He knows small true things: your chassis, your braking point, the bead on a weld. He offers more than he takes, and the offer is the trap.

**How he sounds when he "turns":** he never turns. Phase 3 of the finale is the closest he comes: the only line in the game where he asks someone for something ("Do not round it."), and he asks it of the Voice.

**Sample lines:**
- Paper Night: "A good chassis. I will remember it."
- First receipt: "Four lines. Each one fair. M."
- Levy remark: "The levy keeps the road open. You are welcome to walk instead. M."
- On track: "Your car is cornering well. I have made a note."
- The offer: "I kept your bench. Nobody has touched it in thirteen years. I have already signed my side."
- Seizure: "Clause nine. You will find it on the back of your copy now."
- Finale: "Tell him I kept his bench." / "Do not round it."

## The Mechanic: the kid who apologises to machines

```
CHARACTER: The Mechanic (Ennis), about 22, runs the Yards parts shop under a dead woman's sign
           (Bettany's). Warm brown skin, unruly curls, ochre apron, red shirt; always holding a
           spare bearing. Voiced by Harry (diesel voice).
CORE (his words): "Give me a minute. I can make it honest."
HIDES: The rig under the sheet. That he swept Marrow's garage from six to nine and ran away with a
       bag of bolts. That his shop is on Marrow's paper too. That he does not watch races, because
       he can't.
STATUS MOVE: Defers, then quietly refuses. He will not fit a wrong part, whoever asks.
RHYTHM: Fragments and restarts, but at most ONE hesitation mark per voiced line (the X3 silence
        lesson: mechanic.seizure came back 55% silent). Long lists of parts when nervous;
        very short lines when calm. In the finale he is calm for the first time.
LEXICON: Workshop: bearing, mount, shim, bead, torque, true, seated, thread, honest (of a part),
         "that'll hold". Signature words: "honest", "okay", "sorry" (to machines), "hold on".
         Address: none. He says "you".
GRAMMAR HABITS: Drops subjects ("Swept it twice"). Counts out loud. Corrects himself mid-line.
         Talks to cars in the second person.
NEVER SAYS: a swear word; a lie about a part (he goes quiet instead); "I'm scared", "I'm worried"
         or any named feeling; "trust me"; "boss", "buddy", "pal"; a speech. Never mocks the
         player for losing; he blames the car.
HUMOUR: Self-deprecating and specific; warmth as acts (a swept bay, a kept mirror, a hook at your
        height); deflection into technical detail. Laughs with the player, never at them.
UNDER PRESSURE: More technical, and quieter. Fear shows in his craft (over-checking bolts,
        apologising to the drum), never in a sentence about fear.
ARC: Act 1 nervous fragments -> Acts 2-3 fluent at the bench, nervous anywhere else -> Act 4 the
     pardon in his apron, frightened and quiet -> Act 5 proud of the rig, terrified of using it ->
     finale calm and precise on the radio -> after: takes the mine rack off first.
TELLS: Rolls the bearing in his palm. Counts bolts aloud, twice. Apologises to machines. Listens to
       the Voice on the radio instead of watching. Wipes his feet when Marrow does (and notices).
TTS: Harry, voice SOYHLrjzK2X1ezoPC6cr, eleven_multilingual_v2, stability 0.45, similarity 0.75,
     style 0.25. Nervous helpfulness comes from short breaths and restarts, not tags. Keep the
     silence ratio under 45%: one hesitation per line at most.
```

**What makes him lovable without being a sidekick:** he has his own want, secret, debt, history with the villain, and his own cost. He is the most competent person in the basin, and his kindness is always an act, never a declaration. He sometimes says no to the player. He is funny because he is precise, not because he is the comic relief.

**How he sounds when he turns:** he does not change sides; he changes register. The bravest thing he does is quiet: he keeps Marrow's pardon unsigned, then uses it as a shim.

**Sample lines:**
- Welcome: "Okay. Let me see. Stock tyres, honest axle. I can work with honest."
- The frame: "Don't. It's nobody's. Forty kilos of nobody's. Let it stay that way a bit longer."
- Third wreck in a row: "That's the same bumper bracket as last time. I'm starting to know it personally."
- After the hut: "He kept my bench. Thirteen years. Who keeps a bench?"
- The shim: "I folded it six times. Seven was too thick."
- Duel: "Five seconds a drop. Count with me." / "Don't double back over your own. They don't know whose side they're on."
- Seizure: "Don't look at the empty bay. Look at me. Okay, look at the rig." Later: "I wiped my feet too. When he did. I don't know why I did that."

## The Voice: the announcer who calls what happens

```
CHARACTER: The Voice: the league's announcer for thirty years; with Marrow, one of the two people
           who built the league. Never seen; his booth is the only lit, maintained room at the Crown.
           Voiced by Callum (noir read).
CORE (his words): "I call what happens."
HIDES: He called Tull's death "a driver alone" at the third cairn, because that is what the
       paper said. He has never issued a correction. He is Marrow's only friend, and the only
       person Marrow lets round a number.
STATUS MOVE: Pauses. Lets a fact sit. Never hurries.
RHYTHM: Short declaratives in a noir cadence; long pauses are allowed (one per line); numbers
        rounded ("Twelve Hundred"); sentences often without verbs ("Dusk on the flats. The glare's down. The log is open.").
LEXICON: Broadcast and ledger: "on the board", "the paper", "called", "from the book", "the basin
         is listening", "the Yards", "the flats". Signature words: "from the book", "on the board",
         "the basin", "called". Address: "Twelve Hundred".
GRAMMAR HABITS: Present tense. Says what the player cannot see (who is at stake, what the paper
         says), never what the player can see. Rewards finesse with one dry word.
NEVER SAYS: "ladies and gentlemen", "folks", "welcome back"; exclamation marks; play-by-play ("and
         he crashes"); open sympathy; a side. He reads what is on the paper in front of him,
         which in Act 5 is the crews' paper. That is still not a side.
HUMOUR: Deadpan and noir: "The figure's got some change on it. I don't do change. Twelve Hundred."
UNDER PRESSURE: Flatter. In crown-3 he reads forty minutes of corrections in the voice he uses
        for lap times.
ARC: Dismissive (Act 1) -> grudging (Act 2) -> uneasy (Act 3, after Tull) -> corrective (Act 5:
     "Correction to the record") -> one exact number -> silence. The booth goes dark.
TELLS: "From the book." before every one of Marrow's notices. Rounds every figure. Never says
       Marrow's first name (nobody does).
TTS: Callum, voice N2lVS1w4EtoT3dr4eOWO, eleven_multilingual_v2, stability 0.55, similarity 0.75,
     style 0.30. Noir read: low, dry, unhurried. Commas and full stops direct the pauses; no SSML
     breaks over 1 s.
```

**What makes him frightening, then moving:** for four acts he is the league's legitimacy, the calm voice that makes a death sound like a lap time. In Act 5 the same flatness reads the truth, and that flatness is what makes the truth unanswerable.

**Sample lines:**
- Paper Night: "The figure's got some change on it. I don't do change. Twelve Hundred."
- Act 1 grid: "Ash on the Yards road. Six cars. Twelve Hundred at the bottom of the paper."
- Notice: "From the book: the league thanks Rook for nine years at the gate, in advance."
- crown-3: "Third cairn, two winters back. I called it a driver alone. I was reading his paper. Correction."
- Duel: "Listen for engines. When there's one, I'll say so."
- Last line, to a dead man: "Didn't round it."

## Rook: the collector who owes

```
CHARACTER: Rook, the Ash Yards' gatekeeper and Marrow's collector there. Gaunt, healed cheek scar,
           broken goggle lens on the forehead, dust scarf, one jagged welded shoulder plate.
           Drives a Needle (late-pass specialist), then a Trail after the league takes his Needle.
CORE: "Pay what you owe. That's all a person is."
HIDES: His own key hangs on his own board under the soot. He saw the Mechanic's frame and never
       wrote it down.
STATUS MOVE: Sizes you up, then states the price.
RHYTHM: Clipped, sour, 3 to 9 words. Every line a transaction. Drops articles.
LEXICON: Collection and salvage: tag, hook, weighed, clean tag, sooted, board, gate, pulled, owe.
         Signature words: "hook", "tag", "weighed", "gate". Address: "fresh tag", then "Twelve".
NEVER SAYS: "thank you" (thanks puts you on the hook); "friend"; "trust"; a compliment that isn't
         a calculation.
HUMOUR: Sour, gallows: "That corner collects faster than I do."
UNDER PRESSURE: Counts. Gets formal about rules.
ARC: Hostile collector -> refuses help -> gives away his key -> an ally who still talks in hooks:
     "I'm on your hook now. Don't make it a long one."
TTS: Not cast. A dry, gravelled middle-aged voice; clipped delivery.
```

**Funny or frightening:** frightening at first because he is the system with a face, the man who hangs the keys. Funny later because his morality is all bookkeeping, even his loyalty.

**When he turns:** he keeps his culture (R4 P15). He gives his key, but he would never say "thank you", and he refuses yours.

**Sample lines:** "That corner collects faster than I do." / "Kid, I collect for a living. Don't show me things." / "Nice frame. Never saw it. Now shut your curtain." / "Truck's got my car. Board's got my key. Only one of those I can give away." / Finale: "Nine years I wrote everything down. That frame I left off."

```
CHARACTER: Ox, foreman of the Cinder Row belt crews. Broad, weathered, short silver bristle hair,
           eyebrow scar, enormous slab shoulder armour. Drives a Bastion (close-range bruiser).
           She/her.
CORE: "No one is left on the pour."
HIDES: Her belt builds the house cars. The levy pays for them. She has kept duplicate receipts
       for years and said nothing.
STATUS MOVE: Silence, then an order.
RHYTHM: Short, blunt, declarative; 2 to 8 words; imperatives; counts things.
LEXICON: Foundry and crew: pour, ladle, belt, slag, cooling, roll, crew, plate, shift.
         Signature words: "crew", "pour", "slag", "count". Address: "driver", then "crew".
NEVER SAYS: "I" when she can say "we", except in her confession; a joke at the crew's expense;
         anything about herself unprompted.
HUMOUR: Rare, flat, devastating: "Light car. I'll try to remember you're there."
UNDER PRESSURE: Fewer words. Her car gets heavier on the line (the AI: bruiser).
ARC: Rival protecting jobs -> refuses ("I also know who eats while it stays there") -> confesses
```

**When she turns:** the same bluntness, aimed the other way. She reads the Roll Call before the finale with one extra name: Tull.

**Sample lines:** "Leave a lane, or be one." / "I know what's in my vest. I also know who eats while it stays there." / "I built the cars that chased you. Here's what you paid me for them." / "Cash, a Flint off my belt, or plate cut to your car. The crew's watching how you pick."

## Minor voices

**The Clerk (league clerk; text only, not voiced).** Apologetic bureaucrat. Tags mirrors and posts notices. He apologises, which Marrow never does: "Sorry. It's the form." He appears in cards and the ledger, never as an on-track voice.

**House drivers (equity; optional barks, text only).** Debtors driving league cars, identified by fleet number. They speak in flat call signs, which is creepy because they have no names left: "Fleet nine, on station." "Fleet nine. Contact." Use sparingly; leaders are hunted with no visible cue (owner ruling), so these lines must never announce a hunt. They only react after contact.

**The dissenters (card text only).** A young sorter (Ash Yards), a young hand (Cinder Row), a quarry cutter (Salt Cut), a hut boy (Thin Air), an unpaid pit hand (Crown). One line each, in cards, so that every region disagrees with itself (R4 P24).

## The player: a silent presence

The player never speaks. Silence is a characterisation choice, not a gap.

- **The player acts; others interpret.** Cards use "you" for actions only: "You sign." "You hold the line." They never say what the player thinks or feels.
- **Choices speak:** the payout choice (money, car, part) gets a reaction from the ally and, later, from the next ally ("You took Rook's tyres. Good. Tyres are honest."). Driving speaks: boss lines react to whether the boss finished wrecked or whole.
- **The name is not the player's own.** "Twelve Hundred" is a figure the Voice rounded. Marrow never says it. Mica refuses it. The Mechanic never needs it. By the finale it is just a name; the crews use it without meaning a debt.
- **The ending gives the player a place, not a speech:** a hook by the door, at your height.
