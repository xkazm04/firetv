## Deliverables and structure

Make original instrumental compositions **120–180 seconds** long, with development and contrast rather than a short repeated bed. No lyrics, spoken samples, engine recordings, weapon noises, alarm sounds or countdown beeps: those belong to gameplay. Leave space for the approved engine/effect and narration layers, especially around 1–4 kHz. Prompts describe timbre and arrangement rather than an artist imitation.

Intensity tiers describe sections of a complete song: **T1** is sparse anticipation, **T2** steady driving momentum, **T3** a short peak. A race template is 0–15 s T1 introduction, 15–50 s T2 main idea, 50–75 s T2 contrasting phrase, 75–95 s T1 break, 95–135 s T3 return, then a 10–25 s release. Scale this structure to the chosen 2–3 minute length. Change orchestration, rhythm or harmony across sections; do not simply paste the same eight bars.

The current game has one linear music stream and a loop-capable bed path. It does **not** have a bar-synchronized tier/stem scheduler. Deliver one complete mixed file per cue. Tier notes below are compositional directions, not a promise of adaptive playback. Full songs end cleanly and use `loop: false`. If the owner later wants continuous menu/race playback, provide a **separate** loop edition with measured sample boundaries and a repeat audition; do not mark an unresolved cadence or a fade-to-silence as seamless. Seamless delivery is optional, never a substitute for the full song.

Victory and defeat each need a full 120–180 s resolution composition too. Place a self-contained 4–8 s sting at its opening, followed by a clear decay; the owner can additionally deliver that short excerpt for the one-shot cue. The excerpt, not a three-minute fanfare, is the result sting. Existing approved nonmelodic victory/defeat effects remain until these future music cues are explicitly activated.

## File and loudness contract

Keep the best available **lossless stereo WAV master**, preferably 48 kHz / 24-bit. If the provider delivers a different rate/bit depth, retain that native master and record it; upsampling is not added detail. Deliver the game copy as **stereo Ogg Vorbis, 44.1 kHz, quality 5 or higher**, checked after the final encoding. Music streams through the existing music bus; do not convert full tracks into resident Sound clips.

Match the existing audio bible: **−14 LUFS integrated ±1 LU**, mastering ceiling **−2 dBTP**, final encoded true peak **≤−1 dBTP**, zero clipped full-scale samples. Do not crush all section contrast to hit a number. Short stings need a disclosed short-clip measurement as well as listening; do not pad the delivered audio solely to satisfy a loudness meter. Keep first/last boundaries clean and retain the natural ending tail. Full-song duration excludes an accidental long silent pad.

For every approved file supply cue ID, filename, SHA-256, duration, sample rate, channel count, integrated loudness, true peak and `loop: false`. If a separate loop version is approved, additionally supply start/end-exclusive decoded frame indices, repeat preview and boundary measurements. Keep original exports, edited deliveries and hashes separate. The existing seam screens for loops must remain visible; a normal full-song ending is not a failed seamless loop.

Import later by copying approved files to the paths above, filling each cue's currently empty `path`, `sha256`, `durationSeconds`, `decodedBytes`/stream metadata and owner evidence, populating the manifest's empty `music` list, and changing `musicMode` from `none` to `enabled`. Keep a single stream; legacy `music.rolling`, `music.hunt`, `music.contact` and `music.redline` are compatibility placeholders, not additional commissions. Scene routing and musical transition timing must then be checked on desktop and in an authorized device session. Dropping a file alone does not activate it.
