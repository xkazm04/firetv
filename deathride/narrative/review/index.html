Key lines were chosen by blind scoring with two judges per round; the judges were AI models from the same family as the writer, so your read-aloud choice is the real final step.

FC-01: Cut the living missing sibling

Recommendation: Adopt

What changes. Q0 added a sibling who copied the books, fled the fire and hides behind the Mechanic's shelves. The sibling is removed. The Mechanic's secret becomes the rig alone. The kept ending art's open-identity silhouette now reads as the player coming home.

Why. Not in the owner's plot; the X3 voice wave already chose to follow the owner's plot over the sibling. It spent a reveal on something with no expression in play (R2 P1) and split the Mechanic's secret in two. One reveal per region; cut threads you cannot pay (R1 P16, D12). The investigation-by-card it needed was K1's named risk ("a predictable debt conspiracy stretched over long repeat races").

Cost. S: rewrite story-cards.csv, campaign-beats.csv, the rival-stories.csv Mica biography, and campaign-mechanic.csv act 3 ("Your sibling is safe here"). Art unchanged.
Risk. The owner may have liked the missing-person hook. It is replaced by Paper Night and Tull.

FC-02: Prologue at the Crown: Paper Night

Recommendation: Adopt

What changes. One card before scrap-1: drivers sign Marrow's paper at the Crown, the finale arena. The game ends where the debt began.

Why. An ending lands when it returns to the start, changed (R4 P28, #11). Write the last scene first and set it up from the first (R1 P3). The prologue plants Marrow (reads the car, not the driver), the Voice (rounds the figure) and the empty stands.

Cost. S to M: one new card id with the Crown backdrop. A pre-career card slot is needed; the campaign-victory card shows an extra-card path already exists.
Risk. Front-loaded exposition (R4 P5). Mitigated: one image, three lines.

FC-03: The announcer becomes the Voice, a character with a code and Marrow's one protected bond

Recommendation: Adopt

What changes. The announcer (Callum, noir) has called every race for thirty years. He co-founded the league with Marrow, he reads what is on the paper in front of him, and he never takes a side. Marrow lets only him round numbers. In crown-3 he reads the crews' book on air; Marrow could cut the cable and doesn't. In the finale Marrow's last line is to him: "Do not round it." The Voice answers after the wreck: "Didn't round it."

Why. The villain's one protected relationship, shown wordlessly, charged in the finale (R1 P1, D4; R4 P11). An announcer is a character with a stance, not a play-by-play caller (R2 P13, D6). The villain stays present between fights (R4 P12). It costs no new character. It turns the empty-stands problem into a broadcast story (R4 P27).

Cost. S: lines only. Audio: about 60 new Callum lines when synthesised; 4 recorded lines get proposed replacements (all 14 stay as fallbacks).
Risk. Too subtle for some players; the rounding plant must land (switchback-3, crown-3). The announcer stays neutral by code.

FC-04: The player's name is the starting debt: Twelve Hundred

Recommendation: Adopt

What changes. The Voice rounds the 1,200 credit figure into a name on Paper Night, and the name sticks while the figure changes. Address rules per character: Marrow never says it, Mica refuses numbers and waits for a name, the Mechanic never needs it.

Why. Identity by debt makes the theme (owing against belonging) live in address, not in speeches (R3 A4, address lever; R1 P11). It also solves the silent protagonist's lack of a name.

Cost. S. Voiced "Twelve Hundred" is spelled out.
Risk. If startingDebt changes in balancing, the name stays as the Paper Night figure. Fine in fiction; note it.
