# EM1 · What does my paragraph do?

promotion: discovery

## Goal (user's words)

"Read my paragraph and show me, on the big screen, which sentence is not doing its job and why, so I can fix it myself."

## Definition of done

- The Character sends one paragraph of their own from the phone and, on the TV, sees which sentences do their job and which do not, through a lens they chose.
- The page opens on the sentence that most needs a look, and they can say in their own words why it was marked.
- Every sentence marked as a problem comes with a move and a pattern to fill, and nothing they are shown writes the sentence for them.
- With nothing written yet, they can start from the pattern and get a paragraph read that is entirely their own words.
- When the desk cannot read what they sent (too long, the wrong kind of file, a language it does not read), they find out in a plain sentence before they wait for a reading.

## Levels

- **L1 (always).** A walker over the code: the landing, the lens home, the plan, the forensic page, the playbook and the phone's Essay tab (Surface binding of each Character).
- **L2 through `tools/essay-ui-test.cjs`, partly.** Steps W1 (seat and pair), W2 (Enter on the landing to the lens home, timed), W3 (one paragraph typed into the Essay tab, Evidence lens, Analyse on the TV, timed), W4 (the coached page screenshotted; verdicts, notes, moves, patterns and summary recorded; texts that contain a whole learner sentence reported, not asserted) and W8 (no page errors) bear on it. It does **not** cover: a learner other than its fixed Sam (high-school, 15, `tools/essay-ui-test.cjs:27`) reading its fixed English paragraph (`:28`), so the young voice (Eliška), a Czech text (Kristýna) and Adult mode's Workroom entry (Helena) need the harness extended with a Character's profile and paragraph; any lens but Evidence; a file picked instead of typed; dictation (typing is only `fill`, `:14`); the plan (`essayplan`); a refusal (too long, wrong file); a newly written sentence in a note, which its echo check (`:120-122`) cannot see. It asserts no judgement (`:15-16`): whether a verdict is right is read by a person.
