## W1 ? Feel Lab (2026-09-30)

On the Stick open **Death Ride** (`dev.deathride.tv`). Pair from the TV QR. LEFT/RIGHT on the remote changes the session preset live; the phone's preset button opens the settings sheet. Both displays should agree without a race restart. Balanced is a proposal, not a verdict. Spike retains the original steering coefficients; brake now overrides GO.

For **each of Spike, Loose, Agile, Balanced, Stable**, do these three exercises and label each **too twitchy / right / too heavy**:

1. Slow at the end of the straight and make the tightest hairpin you can. Good: achievable thumb travel and a predictable radius. Bad: running out of pad, delayed turn-in, or an unexpected spin.
2. Slalom left/right down the straight. Good: each direction change arrives when expected. Bad: an oscillation grows, or your thumb must wait for the car.
3. Hold a bend, briefly brake, then countersteer and release. Good: the slide can be caught. Bad: snap-back, uncatchable rotation, or a heavy car refusing the requested turn.

Record preset, maneuver, speed, and the three-word rating. Loose deliberately permits more slip; Stable deliberately softens small corrections. Check simultaneous GO+BRAKE stops propulsion and releasing GO coasts immediately.

Optical check: film phone and TV together at 240 fps for at least 30 FLASH TEST taps. Count frame separation ? 1000/240 ms and record p50/p95/max. Good against the proposed rubric: p50 <=120 ms, p95 <=180 ms. Bad: a growing delay or missed flash. Optical latency and physical-phone comfort are **not measured** by the automated network test.

## D3 - Drift is an input skill; ten-class owner calibration (2026-10-01)

Use **Death Ride Drift Lab** (`dev.deathride.driftlab`, port 8766), Balanced and asphalt first; the integration app is separate. These are proposed exercises, **not an owner-felt approval**. On PC launch `:desktop:run --args=--drift-lab`; C/F/V/X select class/profile/surface/exercise, F1 switches manual/replay, W/A/D/S/Shift drive, G changes slider page, E exports and T restores. The script reports are in [D3 calibration](../docs/concepts/deathride/D3-drift-lab.md).

For each row do three clean attempts, then deliberately miss the catch. Hairpin = Switchback's eastern turn; sweeper = Foundry's broad bend; chicane = Switchback's direction change. Enter at a repeatable speed, compare no handbrake with a short pulse, release and progressively countersteer; avoid learning the pulse while holding full throttle. The pulse hints below come from a 22 m/s open-plane script and are starting comparisons, not compulsory timings or phone-feel evidence.

| Class | Hairpin entry | Long sweeper | Chicane flick |
|---|---|---|---|
| Needle | Compare a brief ~0.5 s pulse with holding twice as long; catch before the nose passes the exit. | Use small throttle/steer changes to keep an angle without a second flick. | Release the first slide before reversing steer; look for quick rotation with a narrow but readable catch window. |
| Line | Start around ~0.6 s and release toward the exit; use as the reference car. | Compare steady throttle with a lift and a light brake; describe each transition. | Make equal left/right inputs and see whether both catches are predictable. |
| Bastion | Begin rotation earlier; compare ~0.8 s with Needle's brief pulse. | Leave room for the heavy slide and begin the catch early; judge whether momentum is useful. | Settle before the second turn; a late reversal should cost line and time. |
| Comet | Set up wide and early, then try ~0.7 s; avoid expecting a tiny-car pivot. | Test the long stable arc at speed and a gradual catch. | Brake before the change and allow its long body room to rotate. |
| Trail | Start around ~0.6 s; compare asphalt then gravel at a lower entry speed. | Try a small lift and reapply throttle; note whether it stays readable. | Use one deliberate flick each way; report bouncing between slide and grip. |
| Flint | Compare ~0.5 s and a slower entry; check the eager rotation despite extra mass. | Try holding moderate angle without repeated handbrake pulses. | Catch the first rotation fully before the second input; watch for snap-back. |
| Quill | Begin with the shortest pulse (~0.4 s), then deliberately overhold. | Use gentle throttle; decide whether its fragile/light role rewards precision. | Try small alternating inputs; flag spins from movements too small to read. |
| Vandal | Start around ~0.6 s, leave a wider exit and catch earlier than Quill. | Compare its continuing momentum with Quill at matched speed. | Ask for the opposite turn early; note sluggishness versus a controllable delay. |
| Kestrel | Set up the widest arc; start around ~0.6 s rather than forcing a tight pivot. | Carry a long smooth slide and gradually restore grip; test whether stability becomes dull. | Slow first and give the long wheelbase time; flag an inability to redirect at reasonable speed. |
| Bulwark | Rotate early with ~0.7 s and leave exit space for the large body. | Catch before the boundary; compare retained momentum with Bastion. | Plan the second turn before adding throttle; mistakes should cost time without a forced control lock. |

Good: the slide starts for a legible reason, speed carries through it, steering/throttle/brake alter its path, an early smooth catch recovers, and a late catch can spin or hit a wall. Distinguish **grabby** (grip returns abruptly), **floaty** (correction has too little effect), **snaps back** (recovery overshoots), **spins too easily** (the usable window is unreadably small), and **cannot hold a slide** (angle dies despite manageable inputs). A heavy should be slower to throw and harder to catch from the same angle; a smaller angle under the same pulse is not proof it is easier in every situation.

Repeat the best/worst class on all five live feel presets, then try asphalt versus gravel/ice. Watch smoke/skids as angle and quality grow; they should explain the slide without hiding the next turn.
