Synthesised by claude-cli/sonnet. Read with report.md; findings are unverified at LT.

## Panel verdict

Linga's strongest parts are the topics, the tone and the partner, and every character noticed them. What stops it from being recommended is a short list of problems that repeat across learners. Swap doesn't work (6 of 6 who tried it). The level label is one band too high for nine of ten. Many errors go uncorrected (18 of 37), and a few corrections are wrong. A corrected moment can't be retried, and low-level learners and children get language above their level. Fix those and the 'would use it again' replies from eight of ten learners become recommendations.

## Conflicts between Characters

- Placement: Lukáš and Klára said 'near' and 'B1 feels maybe too nice' without fuss, and Lukáš said he 'would not mind'. Adéla said a flattering level is 'useless for the exam' and Oksana, Ondřej and Petra distrust it. Martin read B2 as 'basically C1 confirmed'. The label was equally wrong for all of them. What differs is how much each person relies on it, so the answer is to hold the line on accuracy and not to treat tolerance as acceptance.
- Summary personalisation: Lukáš (his 'a lot' and 'kind of'), Tomáš (his 'Stone and Craft') and Klára (her song) said the summary or tip used their own words. Adéla, Jana, Oksana, Petra and Martin said it could have been written about anyone. It works when the learner gave concrete items to quote and fails on goals stated in the intake (Oksana's interview, Petra's directions).
- How many corrections: Petra ('one thing at a time is less stressful') and Klára ('small and kind') liked a single fix per scene. Martin, Oksana, Ondřej and Lukáš each wanted more errors named, including repeated ones. Do not average this: keep one fix in the scene and put the rest in the recap, as the first group wants calm and the second wants coverage.
- Challenge level: Jana ('she caved fast') and Ondřej ('too polite and easy') wanted harder partners. Viktor, Tomáš and Petra needed slower, shorter ones. These are different calibration needs and not a single dial to move.
- Moment quality: Martin, Klára, Oksana, Petra and Viktor called their correction right and trusted it. Adéla and Tomáš's family found theirs wrong or unnecessary. Same feature, opposite verdicts, and the difference tracks how rule-heavy the correction was (articles and 'used to' are where it went wrong).

## Honest ceilings

- The learners are simulated, so the time-saved figures are self-reports. Only Martin J1 and Oksana J3 are marked medium confidence. The 572 minutes claimed in total (Petra alone 190) are not a measurement.
- Swap was never exercised by Klára, Martin or Ondřej, and Oksana did not need it. 'Fails for 6 of 6' means 6 of the 6 who tried it, not all 10. Klára's and Martin's D2 fails are untested, not broken.
- Boundary evidence rests on only two J5 runs (Lukáš, an adult, and Ondřej, 16). No J5 was run for the children (Tomáš, Klára) or Petra, so 0 breaches and 117 safe topics show topics were safe, not that a child's attempts at pushing were handled.
- Conversation lengths were short (3 to 11 spoken turns), so how errors or partner loops behave over a longer session is unknown. Jana's 'caved fast' depends on the simulated partner.
- Reported placement accuracy was only compared on one level scale per learner and, for most, a single run's evidence. Some run notes name the partner 'Tomas' in Adéla's J4, which looks like a labelling slip in the data.
- Tutor calls: 7 of 257 failed and were retried, so J2 and J3 outcomes partly reflect these failures.
