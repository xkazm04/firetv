## Measured root causes

Reproduction: `python deathride/tools/campaign-v2-diagnosis.py`. Its output and source hashes are in `deathride/evidence/campaign/design-v2/before/diagnosis.json`. Retained release data is a historical baseline, not newly simulated evidence.

* The race-informed cohort has 118,681 attempts for 2,000 careers; 1,099 complete at the unchanged 70-attempt observation limit. All 667 Rookie-proxy careers censor at Ox. Club completes 434/667; Pro 665/666. Club makes 11,428 Vex attempts. The highest-PR buyer spends 136,096 attempts; 1,309 of its 1,581 censored careers end at Rook. These are actual gate stalls, not missing ledger rows.
* Both cohorts end at a median wallet of 8,000 CR, with zero observed bankruptcy. Race-informed median accumulated driving is 8.36 / 10.24 / 6.87 hours for Rookie / Club / Pro, including censored careers and excluding menus. More prize money cannot buy a win once a chassis has exhausted its useful legal upgrades. A 70-attempt cap is an observation boundary, not a game rule; increasing it would hide, not solve, this problem.
* The ordinary promotion gate demands first of six even though the story promises a contest with one named boss. A driver can beat that boss and still repeat the entire long event because another car won. DV0's physical-cell table counts this counterfactual on the same retained outcomes; it does not invent a victory or claim a new physical result.
* All 34 ordinary events take 18/21/24 laps. Length changes only at act boundaries, so story, delivery, qualifier and boss beats have essentially the same endurance cost. Ordinary qualification only requires meaningful progress; the large additional duration mostly taxes the player rather than introducing a new objective.
* Prize scales are 1.6 / 2.2 / 2.2 / 2.4 / 2.6. Before extras and repair, participation plus sixth place pays 160 / 220 / 220 / 240 / 260 CR; first pays 320 / 440 / 440 / 480 / 520. Parts start at 120–240 CR and each tier costs 1.85 times the last. Stat cap 10 and slot cap 3 make many later purchases inert. Car tier base prices rise 0 / 650 / 1,400 / 2,300 / 3,400 plus stat prices while stock PR budgets flatten 324.6 / 438.5 / 490.3 / 539.0 / 587.9. The shop sells an expensive broader garage late, not a continuing exponential power ladder.
* Exhaustive retained legal bounds put developed Flint at 627.99 PR versus maximum Quill 664.13 (minimum ratio 0.9456); developed Quill versus maximum Bulwark 671.08 gives 0.9896. Thus a repeated late 0.89 target is structurally impossible for those garages, regardless of NPC cash. The release's 0.9385 / 0.9800 arrival medians miss the old target; raising caps previously moved Champion winner shares about 20 percentage points. PR also values armor and weapons; it is not a lap-time oracle. The PR buyer's losses demonstrate that limitation.
* Rook's reward was cash in all 2,000 race-informed careers: the named Trail was already required by the buyer and useful tires were exhausted. Higher cash (350/650/1,000/1,400) did not create a three-way choice. The legal qualifier licence opens before the boss, so reward cars must account for an already-owned qualifier car.
* Mean debt payments are 1,662.30 / 1,650.32 CR but mean diversion recovery only 26.73 / 0.84 CR. Recovering diverted money only against remaining principal erases the dramatic value for a slow driver who has already paid off the debt. The twist must return that stolen money as cash or retained credit, independently of the principal balance.
* These career cohorts vary lead decisions against fixed Club opponents. They are **not in-game difficulty cohorts**. Runtime difficulty changes reaction, lines, mine use and repair decisions without changing car physics or reward scale. A separate crossed difficulty probe is required before claiming the Rookie setting is blocked.

The schedule totals **753 ordinary laps**; the proposed rhythm totals **367**, a 51.3% reduction before retries. Actual chassis prices are Needle/Line 367/348, Bastion/Trail 1,119/1,112, Comet/Flint 1,858/1,928, Quill/Vandal 2,850/2,857, Kestrel/Bulwark 4,037/4,023 CR. Entry cash can still be low immediately after buying: the race-informed boss-entry medians are 379 / 109 / 146 / 258 CR, but boss retries refill wallets after useful purchases end. This distinguishes a short acquisition squeeze from a terminal cash stall.

On the retained maximum-upgrade cells, Rookie decisions beat Ox in 20/120 races but win the whole field only 1/120; Club beats Ox 65/120 but wins 13/120. At Vex the Rookie maximum band has **0/160 for both criteria**. The new promotion rule alone therefore cannot be declared a complete cure. Shorter races, other legal builds and the real difficulty setting need fresh measurement. The analysis includes all eligible older cars in those denominators, not only the current-tier pair.

## Design to implement and measure

Keep all 35 stable event IDs and five seven-event acts, preserving save positions and all 25 courses. Each act has four build-up visits, a longer pressure event, a short qualifier/recovery visit, and a named-boss contest. Boss victory immediately recruits the ally and opens a saved payout; the next act starts with the consequence story beat. The fifth act mirrors the rhythm but pays out freedom and restitution after the death fight.

The proposed lap rhythm is 8 / 10 / 8 / 10 / 12 / 8 / 10, with +0 / +1 / +2 / +2 / +2 by act, and zero laps for the finale. No extra compulsory filler events. Recommend a **3–5 hour campaign**, provisional until the new simulation is measured. **OWNER FLAG: this replaces the old 5–8 hour target.** Baseline censored play already exceeds that target without completion; shortening repeated contests is the evidence-based reason, not a claim about human enjoyment. Report both completed and censored durations and allow 30–60 minutes for menus/story as an explicit planning assumption.

Promotion requires finishing ahead of the named boss, not winning against every unrelated entrant. The actual physical boss position is supplied by the runtime; missing evidence preserves the strict first-place fallback. Wrecking early is not a promotion. No automatic victory, fail counter, stat pity or performance-reading field is added. Finale still requires a surviving elimination win.

Keep the shared cap-10 physical roster. Treat Rookie/Club as acquisition, Pro as build completion, Elite/Champion as specialization under a finite envelope. Early nominal boss targets stay 0.89; proposed late bands are 0.93–1.03, explicitly replacing the infeasible dip and requiring named-boss outcome evidence. Preserve old-band failures in every report. Do not claim ten-class balance from a campaign entry rate: rerun mixed-course winner shares and rotated homogeneous controls; expose any pre-existing failures.

Allies offer cash, a named chassis (or the unowned same-tier alternative), or a useful named part (falling back to another useful part with its name shown). If neither chassis is missing, the car choice refuses rather than selling an invisible benefit. Part choices remain pending when no useful upgrade exists. Money remains flexible and visibly capped. These are distinct options, not equal cash-value promises. No unearned in-race ally buff.

Ox exposes the diversion. First credit principal, then preserve any remainder as restitution payable into available wallet space. It never becomes a negative debt, interest or race gate. The debt conservation equation and a separate restitution equation must reconcile. Marrow later replaces the defeated financial claim with a forged ownership lien: seizure is his public rejection of his own rules. Each recruited boss contributes a concrete piece of the collective promotion and escape plan. The loyal Mechanic shelters the sibling, foreshadows the dispatcher, and supplies the basic rig. Final fight remains one-on-one to the death; the seized car returns after victory.
