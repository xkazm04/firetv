# C4 The Ash Circuit — design first, 2026-10-01

Thirty-five events, seven per act: Scrap League / The Yards / Rook; Foundry Cup / Foundry row / Ox; Salt Flats Series / flats and quarry / Vex; Switchback Circuit / mountain road / Mica; The Crown / Marrow's speedway / Marrow. Preserve Rook, Ox, Mica, Vex and Relay, add Marrow, and give each an original biography, taunts and portrait key. Three-line story cards are optional menu text with P4 backdrop keys; reading never gates a race. No image generation or artwork changes.

The new profile contains six rival garage profiles using the same ownership, credits, parts, car prices, insurance and PowerRating functions as the player. Initial wealth and sponsor grants are declared data. Rival purchases happen in named driver shopping windows and stay under a fixed event PR target; neither the target nor the grant reads player PR, wins or skill. Actual rival finishing position, damage and kills settle through Economy, so wins fund earlier purchases and wreck repairs set them back. Grudges remember player-caused wrecks and alter passing/fire decisions only, never physics/HP/damage. All difficulty settings share field/shop/reward content; only decision skill changes.

`career-curve.csv` owns the per-event field target, desired ratio, money multiplier and sponsor grant. The report must publish actual field PR, purchased player PR, their ratio and actual income/next-afford prices, not relabel targets as measurements. Compare a declared family of legal spending policies and choose the best measured policy within that family; do not claim a proof of globally optimal play. Simulate 2,000 seeded careers from an actual fixed-step outcome library, with sample coverage and approximation limits explicit. Check the 2–3 / 7–9-race milestones, joins, boss dips, finale ratio, bankruptcy and early-wreck rate.

The finale has two entrants: player and Marrow. Inactive slots cannot collide, shoot, receive pickups, rank or end the race. A connected second player spectates without consuming an item or earning a fictitious result. Minimal host/controller eligibility, telemetry and menu bindings expose the new core flow; rendering style and all art files stay untouched.

Version 4 migration retains v1–v3 cash, ownership, parts, inventory/debt and trophies. Map old 12-round progress proportionally into 35 events, preserve partial cup points as explicit migration credit until the next cup settles, and retain four earned medals in the corresponding five-cup slots. Rival profiles are nested validated records, not filesystem identities or extra player save files. New campaigns carry a small declared inherited debt; old saves do not acquire retroactive debt.

Keep the approved three-lap race format initially. Measure total active racing time and report the proposed 5–8 hours as a missed target if the content cannot support it; do not invent menu time or pad the race with waits. If evidence warrants a format change, record its structural cost before changing laps. Human story quality, tension, fairness and whether the climb is worth it remain owner-only. Required closure: content/behavior/migration tests, full green build, raw pacing evidence and four-curve plot, /24 scan and Stick career/story/rival check, OWNER-CHECKS/status/session entry and one C4 commit.


## Implementation and declared experiment

The fixed schedule grants every rival its own sponsorship amount once per new event. Regular shopping windows differ by driver; division joins and bosses always allow a visit. Purchases pay the same prices and use the same part unlocks, trade recovery, borrowing limits and stat caps as the player. A target is an upper purchase limit, not a command to manufacture PR: lack of funds, discrete parts and class caps can leave the measured field below it. Each difficulty uses the same schedule and prices. Actual race performance can change NPC wealth across difficulties without changing physics or granting catch-up power.

The player may enter with an owned lower-tier car; a car above the current division is rejected. This deliberate exception to a course's competitive field minimum prevents a cash shortage from locking the career. NPCs obey the course pool. Practice remains unrestricted. The final duel excludes Player 2 from its race ticket and from replacing Marrow; spectator inventory and money must remain unchanged.

The physical library covers every event, three opponent skill settings, every player class at or below the division, six legal purchase caps (0/1/2/4/8/17 installed parts), and eight seeds: 30,240 actual races. A Club-skill AI drives the lead car. Stop when that lead finishes or wrecks, matching the one-human host's settlement boundary; unfinished rivals receive their actual position/condition at that boundary. Replay seed zero in every cell. A canonical rival economy evolves using the actual reference outcome for each event, with its cursor explicitly moved through all 35 events for coverage; this reference is not claimed as an earned playthrough.

For each setting, 2,000 separately seeded careers use the real shop, unlock, insurance, debt, trophy and rival-settlement code. Choose the nearest available player/field PR-ratio band for the current owned car and sample a physical result from that cell. Report the approximation gap. Rival garages in each career receive those sampled race facts and subsequently shop for themselves, so their exact field configurations can differ from the canonical physical field. This is the W7 physical-library approximation, not 2,000 independently simulated complete campaigns.

Compare caps of 1/2/3/4/6/8/17 installed parts over 64 pilot careers per setting and policy. Prefer completed careers, then mean purchased PR. Buy successive tiers in order, save for the next tier when it unlocks, and wait for the best available PR-per-credit offer instead of buying a nearly inert cheap brake. After a failed final, the policy allows another installed part per retry. This is the best measured member of a declared policy family, not a proof of globally optimal play. Curves use first visits to events; retry-weighted hours and completion/censor counts are reported separately. Money records gross and net earned income and the banked amount, the next useful PR-per-credit upgrade, next-tier car cost after trade and the additional races to afford them after the current wallet (zero when already affordable; -1 when the measured income is zero and the wallet is insufficient).

The original lower reward pilot produced a late/occasionally skipped Club purchase under actual poor finishes, despite looking acceptable with synthetic third-place income. The retained calibration evidence motivated common act reward scales 1.6 / 2.2 / 2.9 / 3.7 / 4.5, sequential-tier spending and larger fixed boss shop limits. No car stat, difficulty multiplier or damage value was altered to fit the pacing sample. Initial calibration includes one seed per physical cell and is explicitly preliminary. The first full four-band sample is retained separately; its early wallet saturation prompted the fixed-award correction and the wider spending-policy sample.

Version 2 earned ownership uses the old unlock table before proportional progress mapping; version 3 ownership remains exact. This matters for Trail, earned after old round two but not yet newly unlocked at mapped round five. Version 4 recursively validates six bounded non-nested rival records. Ally/grudge/neutral relationships are visible text flags; only a grudge changes passing/fire decisions. No image or rendering style changed; the host changes bind story/menu text and hide inactive duel slots.

A repeat season grants the original Line entry car only if no rookie car remains owned. The Champion garage and its parts stay owned; re-entering Scrap must not require selling the earned top-tier car. This explicit completion award is not applied as a retroactive debt or property replacement during migration.


Late-import regression: v2/v3 saves receive a declared NPC-only genesis budget of 1,800 credits per already-reached act plus the normal 190 starting credits. This does not invent old wins, read player wealth or change player money. A required class replacement is performed even when the ordinary part-shopping window is closed. Tests cover all twelve old round positions and repeat-season entry. Completed legacy seasons also receive a rookie entry car if none remains owned.

Fixed cup awards, contract pay, clean/streak bonuses and pickup cash now pay their stated credit amounts. Division multipliers apply only to the ordinary participation/position/wreck-bounty prize. Previously the new C4 multiplier also multiplied fixed awards, disagreeing with the menu and filling wallets prematurely. Final authored prize scales are 1.6 / 2.2 / 2.9 / 3.1 / 3.3; fixed cup bonuses grow as their own data, without a second multiplier. The monetary trace records gross prize, repair, debt repayment, net earned, banked income and any amount discarded at the 8,000-credit cap. The plot shows earned income before that cap; races-to-afford also accounts for the current wallet.

A fully funded shop diagnostic establishes the attainable boss fields under the actual part unlocks, stat caps and purchase rule. A field target is not relabeled as its achieved rating. Stronger player policies may make the proposed boss ratio unattainable with these peer cars; report that limitation rather than injecting hidden rival stats. A policy never buys a zero/negative PR change solely to reach its part cap.


Two-player binding check: ordinary guests must use an owned car within the host division or below, and receive the same ordinary division prize multiplier. Their own career does not advance and they receive no host cup award. The final spectator remains exempt from entry-car validation and receives no ticket. The Stick guest probe exercises a rejected unowned Champion, a corrected Line selection, two actual controller streams, reserve consumption and a payout independently calculated from the CSV prize rules. The pacing sample itself remains one player versus the authored AI field.

Baseline policy excludes optional player loans, contracts, reserves and manual service. It measures progression from ordinary racing under default insurance. The separate C3 tests/device checks cover those features; no claim is made that this baseline searches every possible financial or combat strategy.

## Measured result and remaining pacing work

The final evidence is [c4-measured](../../../deathride/evidence/phase2/c4-measured/), with [the four curves](../../../deathride/evidence/phase2/c4-measured/pacing.png), raw compressed physical/career/timeline CSVs, independent audit, policy comparison, source hashes and build logs. Earlier experiments remain in `c4-calibration`; their outcomes are not the final content result. No Grok calls or spending occurred in C4.

All 30,240 physical races resolved within the existing 180-second boundary. The report replayed 3,780 cells, and a separate final-runtime run reproduced all 3,780 corresponding physical rows exactly, including hashes and rival outcomes. The independent Python audit reconstructed all 214,470 sampled settlements from the raw facts and CSV rules: position prizes, cup awards, clean/streak pay, repair, debt and wallet cap. All 6,000 sampled careers completed; none were censored or bankrupt and none had a first-lap lead wreck. These are AI-reference measurements, not human fairness certification.

Milestone values are races from career start. The declared policy comparison selected eight installed parts per car: mean purchased PR 567.13 over its 192 pilot careers, compared with 553.05 for four parts and 566.57 for seventeen. Four parts completed faster (35.42 versus 35.71 races). The selection objective favors PR after completion; it is neither a fastest-career claim nor an exhaustive optimal strategy.

Club-reference P/F is 0.8966 at the opening; 0.9245, 0.9930, 0.9984 and 0.9996 at the first four bosses; and 1.0570 at the final duel. The opening and finale proposals pass; the proposed 0.85–0.90 boss dips do not. Real part purchases create visible sawtooth relief, but the later bosses are approximately peers by PR. A funded diagnostic using this exact greedy shop reaches boss fields 490.36 / 587.16 / 635.10 / 649.17 / 637.35 against targets 505 / 620 / 655 / 705 / 635. Those are outcomes of the purchase rule, not a proof of maximum possible PR. Raising a target alone cannot manufacture a stronger car.

The first-part 2–3-race proposal passes Club/Pro and is faster on Rookie. The first Club car is within 7–9 races on every setting. Subsequent mean intervals include 6.41–6.89 races as delayed buyers catch up to the fixed division schedule; some miss the proposed 7–9 interval. The audit compares interval boundaries at two decimal places and retains unrounded means. 15,139 of 214,470 sampled race ratios (7.06%) lie outside the proposed 0.8–1.15 envelope, chiefly relief from upgrades. The largest physical-library ratio mismatch is 0.06349 (95th percentile 0.05032); first-visit event mean mismatch stays below 0.05. This approximation is material when interpreting small PR differences.

The monetary trace separates earned income from banking: 680,452 credits in aggregate were discarded at the wallet cap, averaging 113.41 per career. The Champion next-tier price becomes zero because no higher tier exists; a zero useful-upgrade price means the current policy has no positive-PR offer. It does not mean all shop items are free.

At the four division joins, Club-reference field PR falls 2.98%, 9.22%, 9.08% and 7.75% from the preceding boss. Player PR changes +0.86%, -8.39%, -1.28% and +3.29% as upgraded old cars are traded for new chassis. All sampled careers get through these joins, but those visible steps are not a smooth monotonic stats ramp. The report retains them explicitly for the next tuning pass rather than treating completion alone as proof of a good transition.

**The proposed five-to-eight-hour duration is not achieved.** At 35 events, five hours requires 8.6 active minutes per event and eight hours requires 13.7, beyond this three-lap format and its three-minute race limit. Remaining design work is a longer race/series format and its pacing validation, plus stronger boss purchase/field composition and tier-join tuning through legal shared stats. No menu time, compulsory retry padding or hidden rival power was added to make the target appear green. Current progression is playable and bounded; full pacing acceptance remains open.
