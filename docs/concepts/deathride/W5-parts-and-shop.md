## Structure before prices

Pools: credits and installed part tiers per car. Faucets: participation, finish-position bonus, bounded wreck bounty. Sinks: automatic pit service and one-way part purchases. No resale, interest, random loot or currency conversion. The reinforcing loop is finish -> credits -> parts -> performance -> finish, with one race of latency. Balancing boundaries are finite part tiers, stat/class limits, a modest finish-reward spread, a bounty cap and a wallet cap. A participation floor plus insured pit-service cap means even a wreck with zero starting cash can start the next race fully repaired. Repairs are paid from that race's gross prize; the results screen itemizes the charge and insurance. There is no debt or entry fee.

Run a deterministic unit-rate walk before authoring coefficients. Compare the uncapped reinforcing loop with the proposed finite tiers/cash cap; record every pool and whether the purchase/repair drains actually fire. The bounded walk does not prove balance. It explicitly exposes that a finite garage eventually runs out of purchases; the wallet cap prevents unbounded accumulation, while a completed garage is an end-state, not an infinite live economy.

After the structural walk, author prices/rewards in CSV and sweep plausible reward magnitude, repair rate, part prices, wreck frequency and finish advantage separately. Report net income, first earned upgrade and final wallet/tiers across seeded economic careers, including permanent last-place/wreck and permanent-winner policies. Frequencies in this economic model are assumptions; actual combat-driven campaign pacing is W7/W8. Do not describe that abstract model as played race evidence.

## Parts and interaction with class

Six families: engine raises Speed/Acceleration; tires raise Grip; brakes raise a new **Braking** primary stat; armor raises Armor/Mass while costing Handling; suspension raises Grip/Handling; mounts raise Weapon slots. Stock Braking is identical across all classes, preserving the current stock brake force; its new mapping lets a brake purchase have a distinct mechanical effect without secretly changing the approved steering presets. The other seven W2 stats remain. All coefficients, maximum tiers, prices and descriptions are data.

Parts belong to the selected car, so a tuned Line does not silently upgrade Bastion. Each upgrade replaces the previous tier and adds its declared cumulative stat deltas. Offers show before/after stats, price and next tier; a class at the affected positive-stat limit cannot pay for a useless upgrade (for example Trail tires or Bastion mounts). Final stats clamp through the same W2 mapping, including the separate mount cap. Core tests drive upgraded specs and prove braking distance, acceleration, grip/armor and ammo/weapon availability actually change.

## Garage and save behavior

TV PLAY/PAUSE opens the garage from lobby/results; UP/DOWN selects a family, LEFT/RIGHT selects a car, SELECT purchases the shown tier, BACK returns. The phone's GARAGE sheet shows its own profile, installed tiers, cash and an explicit buy button. Commands carry the expected tier so a repeated request cannot buy the next tier accidentally. Buying while racing is rejected. Both surfaces read the same core offers and receipts.

Profiles use a stable browser-generated identifier separate from the reconnect seat token; the unpaired TV has a couch profile. The save stores selection, per-car tiers, credits, starts/settlements and the last receipt. Starting a paid race reserves a monotonically increasing ticket; settling that ticket is idempotent. Lobby demos and abandoned races do not earn money. Saves use a versioned, validated, checksummed format, a synced temporary file, atomic replacement where supported and a previous-good backup. Corrupt primary recovery is visible; two corrupt copies disable persistence rather than quietly discarding the owner's progress. All writes happen outside simulation steps.

Validation: content/offer rules, real upgraded movement/combat effects, duplicate purchase and settlement rejection, seeded economy extremes/sweeps, codec corruption/truncation/range checks and backup recovery. Core/link/APK green, then on-Stick remote and touch purchases, cash/tier agreement and process-restart persistence. Owner value/choice/comfort remain not measured.

## Implementation and measured model results

Six data-defined families now feed the actual car stat mapping. Stock braking remains 22 m/s?; each brake tier adds one point and one m/s?. The shop displays positive deltas and armor's handling cost. Tier prices use `ceil(basePrice * 1.85^(tier-1))`; class caps reject useless purchases. All five cars have independent installed parts. Stable phone profile IDs survive process death, while seat tokens remain session-specific. Main saves and previous-good backups are checksummed and validated; invalid profile paths are rejected. The shop publishes a change only after the save succeeds, and rejected/stale offers do not write a file. Practice starting/aborting does not settle a receipt.

The unit-rate topology walk is preserved in `w5-structure.csv`, including each faucet and a greater-than-half inflow flag: the uncapped reinforcing finish faucet dominates; the bounded toy walk limits it to half. The walk was first run before coefficients; the explicit faucet-share columns were added during review. The coefficient model then runs **2,000 seeded 100-race careers per scenario**, 13 scenarios / 2.6 million abstract results. This is an economic sensitivity model, not millions of simulated physical races. Baseline first *earned* purchase is 1.7025 races, mean net 108.87 CR/race, final cash 6,047.74 CR, 15 useful Line tiers. A permanent last-place wreck still earns 40 CR, buys its first part after three races, reaches 13 tiers and ends with 429 CR; zero bankruptcies. Permanent winners reach the 8,000 CR wallet cap and 15 useful tiers. The 160 CR starter grant separately permits an immediate brake or tire purchase. Finite completion and capped cash are explicit; no claim of an endless balanced economy is made.

The tornado ranks absolute final-cash swing: reward size **4,883.47 CR**, repair rate **2,460.69**, prices **2,220.98**, assumed upgrade advantage **1,145.17**, assumed wreck frequency **841.48**. Reward ?20% moves first earned purchase from 2.152 to 1.4245 races, so the selected reward scale meets the intended early purchase window without increasing it. An initial model rounded fractional upgrade advantage away; this was corrected to a seeded probabilistic fractional place gain before using the report. Assumed player outcome frequencies remain unmeasured. CSVs record the ranges and their basis.

Core tests cover each part's consumer, actual braking distance, armor and heavy-ammo changes, per-car ownership, duplicate/stale requests, insured receipts, capped wallet, save round-trip and corruption/backup recovery. Link tests reject purchases during races or for another profile, and bound pending purchases to one. Phone and TV share core offers; the phone menu supports vertical scrolling on small landscape screens. `w5-build.txt` records the green core/link/APK task set.
